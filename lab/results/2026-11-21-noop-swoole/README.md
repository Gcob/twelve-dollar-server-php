# First number: the no-op Swoole server

Written in two steps. The **Prediction** section below was written before any measured run of this series (the first
run of the series started after the file existed). Everything after it was written once the runs were done.

## Prediction

Prior art, as claims to check, not as results:

- **mattsplat:** about 18 µs/req, closed loop, 64 connections, no think time, 1 Apple Silicon core, with the whole API
  (mostly SQLite reads). A no-op does no SQLite and no JSON of rows, so it should cost less than 18 µs/req.
- **tzimpel:** 50 to 60 µs/req at a fixed rate on the real mix on Apple Silicon. A fixed rate costs more per request
  than a closed loop (fewer requests per `epoll_wait`, more wake-ups), so the no-op at a fixed rate could land above
  mattsplat's closed-loop number.

Our prediction for the no-op server (`lab/servers/noop-swoole.php`, image `8.3-apt`, 1 CPU):

1. **8 to 20 µs/req at 3,000 req/s.**
2. **Higher per request at 1,000 req/s than at 6,000 req/s**: fixed costs per wake-up are spread over fewer requests.
3. **Peak RSS under 50 MB** with a few hundred connections.


## What was measured

The no-op Swoole server (`lab/servers/noop-swoole.php`: 200 `{"status":"ok"}` to every request, no route, no database,
one worker, `SWOOLE_BASE`) in image `php-lab:8.3-apt`, loaded by k6 at a fixed request rate with `lab/macro.sh`.

- **Rates:** 1,000, 3,000 and 6,000 req/s. **30 s measured after a 10 s warm-up.**
- **3 repeats, interleaved** (1k, 3k, 6k, 1k, 3k, 6k, 1k, 3k, 6k), because the host drifts: an earlier session saw a
  35 % climb in 6 minutes with the same command.
- **µs CPU per request** = the server container's `usage_usec` delta in `cpu.stat` over the measured run, divided by the
  requests k6 sent in it. It is read from outside the cgroup by a third container, so a read is never part of the number.
- All 9 runs are **VALID**: error rate 0 %, 0 dropped iterations, achieved rate within 0.02 % of the requested one.
- **This is an arm64 number, in Docker Desktop on an Apple M3 Pro, not the bench VM.** Relative results (rate against
  rate) count on this Mac; absolute numbers only count on x86 on the bench VM.

Reading the columns:

- **connections open** is the number of established sockets on the server's port, the maximum of 3 samples during the
  run. It equals the VUs k6 pre-allocated (rate / 10, at least 50). **VUs in flight** is only the peak of iterations
  running at one instant, not a connection count.
- **memory.peak** counts page cache, **VmHWM** (the PHP process's peak resident set) does not. Both cover the whole life
  of the server container, warm-up included, not only the measured run.
- The server was never throttled by its CPU limit (`server_throttled_usec` is 0 in all 9 runs). It used 5 to 13 % of its
  CPU over the window; its idle cost between runs was 49 to 149 µs per second, so idle time is not in the number.
- k6 ran at 31 %, 64 % and 89 % of one CPU at 1k, 3k and 6k, on 8 CPUs it can spread over, so it was not the limit either.

## Did the prediction hold?

**1. "8 to 20 µs/req at 3,000 req/s": did not hold.** The median at 3k is **30.16 µs/req** (29.90 to 30.44 over the 3
runs), 51 % above the top of the range. The three runs are within 2 % of each other, so this is not noise: the whole
range sits below the measured values. No rate we ran reached it either: the lowest, at 6k, is 21.66 µs/req (21.42 to
22.42), just above 20.

**2. "Higher per request at 1,000 than at 6,000": held, and the effect is larger than the prediction implied.** Medians:
50.20 µs at 1k, 30.16 at 3k, 21.66 at 6k. Per request, 1k costs 2.3 times 6k, and every 1k run (48.16, 50.20, 56.83) is
above every 3k run, which is above every 6k run. In CPU per second the server costs 50.2 ms/s at 1k, 90.5 ms/s at 3k
and 130.0 ms/s at 6k: six times the requests for 2.6 times the CPU. A straight line through the 1k and 6k points gives
about 16 µs per extra request plus about 34 ms of CPU per second that does not depend on the rate (the 3k point lies
about 10 % above this line, so it is only a rough fit with three points). The part that does not depend on the rate is not idle
time (49 to 149 µs/s measured between runs); that it comes from per-wake-up work is a hypothesis, not checked here. It is also confounded with the connection
count in this series: the rate and the connections rise together (100, 300 and 600 connections at 1k, 3k and 6k), so a part that
does not depend on the rate could depend on the connections instead. The 2,000 req/s runs in `lab/README.md` ("VUs and
connections", 50, 200 and 500 connections, no visible effect on µs/req) point to connections not mattering, but they were taken
at one rate and are not a test of this.

**3. "Peak RSS under 50 MB with a few hundred connections": held.** At 300 connections (3k): **VmHWM 28.73 MiB** (about
30.1 MB), `memory.peak` 15.25 MiB. At 600 connections (6k): 30.11 MiB and 17.84 MiB. The server's peak grew by 2.6 MiB
(VmHWM) from 100 to 600 connections.

**The prior art, for context.** mattsplat's 18 µs/req was a closed loop on the whole API; our fixed-rate no-op is above it
at 3k (30.16) and at 6k (21.66). That fits tzimpel's remark that a fixed rate costs more per request than a closed
loop, but we did not run a closed loop, so this run does not test the remark. Our number for this server is the one measured above, not 18.

## Surprising or worth knowing

- **The 3k and 6k runs did not drift.** 29.90, 30.16, 30.44 at 3k and 21.66, 21.42, 22.42 at 6k, over 6 minutes. The
  35 % climb seen before in 6 back-to-back runs at 2,000 req/s did not show here. The 1k runs spread more (48.16 to
  56.83, 18 %, with the high one in the middle of the series, not at the end): no direction either.
- **One tail outlier:** run 3 at 6k had max latency 82.86 ms and p99 3.91 ms, against max latencies of 22.85 and 27.28 ms
  and p99 of 1.52 and 1.43 ms in runs 1 and 2. It dropped no iteration (600 VUs absorbed the stall) and its µs/req (22.42) is the
  highest of the 6k runs, but only 4 % above the median. Where the stall happened (server, VM, host) is not known.
- **The latency barely moves with the rate:** p95 0.49 to 0.67 ms and p99 1.04 to 3.91 ms at all three rates, medians
  0.52 to 0.61 and 1.48 to 1.77. It includes k6's own scheduling and 100 to 600 connections.
- **k6 costs more than the server at 6k:** 89 % of one CPU against 12.8 % for the server (k6 on 8 CPUs). Harmless here
  (k6 has its own CPUs), but the load generator is the heavier side of this setup.
