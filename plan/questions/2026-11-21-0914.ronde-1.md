### Q6/6 [runtime, rules] : Workerman's parse caches

You asked to check rule 5 before spending time on Workerman (D6). I read Workerman 5.2.4: its HTTP layer keeps 5
static caches between requests, and none can be switched off.

- `TcpConnection` keeps up to 512 **decoded `Request` objects**, keyed by the whole raw request (up to 2 KB). An
  identical request, from any connection, skips parsing entirely and gets a clone.
- `Http::input` caches the header length, and `Request` caches parsed headers, query strings and bodies (256 each).

None of them holds data from SQLite, a response or a JWT result, so the letter of rule 5 holds. But they're caches
between requests, and they shine on a load test where thousands of VUs send byte-identical `GET /feed` requests. A
reviewer could call it rule 5 or rule 7 ("nothing that only works because it's a benchmark").

**In short:** stock Workerman is a grey zone; we either remove the caches or skip its HTTP layer.

**Question:** how do we run Workerman in the bake-off?

- **A.** Patch the caches out in `build.sh` (like tzimpel's Swoole patch), and measure that clean Workerman. We can
  also run it once with the caches, just to see what they buy.
  **Aims at:** measuring Workerman as a real runtime, the way you asked (D6).
  **For:** a clean, submittable Workerman, and its PHP parser against Swoole's C one. **Against:** a patch to keep
  working on each Workerman version.
- **B.** Use only Workerman's event loop and connections, with our own HTTP protocol class. Its HTTP layer never runs.
  **Aims at:** spending our time on our own parser (runtime 3 of D6).
  **For:** no grey zone at all, and the DIY parser gets tested on two loops (Workerman's and raw ext-event).
  **Against:** we never learn what Workerman's own parser costs.
- **C.** Drop Workerman from the bake-off.
  **Aims at:** the shortest bake-off.
  **For:** half a day saved. **Against:** we lose the only PHP-userland comparison with Swoole.

**Confidence:** A 55 % · B 35 % · C 10 %

**Recommendation:** A: I put measuring Workerman as it really is first, as you asked. A and B are close: B would
fold into the DIY runtime.

**Flips if:** once patched, Workerman's parser can't come within 30 % of Swoole on a no-op route (then its loop with
our parser, B, is the only Workerman worth having).

**Answer:** A
