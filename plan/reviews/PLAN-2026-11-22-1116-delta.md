## 🟠 MAJOR

1. **D41's live stream can fall behind, its warning sign is wrong, and its fallback doesn't help.**
   - **What k6 does with a slow reader:** k6's outputs buffer samples in memory and flush on an interval, so a FIFO reader that falls behind doesn't slow the VUs. k6's memory grows instead, and D39's `--memory` limit kills k6 in the middle of a staircase.
   - **The wrong warning sign:** "k6 blocks on the FIFO, seen as a lower achieved rate at low load" (`details/phase-4.md:155-159`) can't show it. The lag only appears at high rates, and as k6 memory, not as rate.
   - **The useless fallback:** one scenario per step streams exactly as many CSV rows as one scenario.
   - **The calibration misses the stream:** D39 measures k6's memory per VU without the CSV output, so the cap is too high.
   - **The volume:** about 12 rows per request, about 240,000 rows/s at 20,000 req/s (`decisions.md` D39, D41).

## 🟡 MINOR

2. D39's cap has no fd term. Below about 0.8 MiB per VU, the memory formula allows more than 65,535 − 60,000 − the server's own fds (about 15, `conns-diy-8.5.txt`), and a starved server fails the step on fds, not CPU. Cap at min(memory cap, about 5,400) (`details/phase-4.md:140-145`).
3. D43's fallback, "the fold reads it that way" (`details/phase-4.md:221-224`), isn't a mechanism. If C frames land on the caller, S6, S7 and S8 can't be split inside a route function. A thin userland wrapper per step (`s6_execute()`, `s7_encode()`) would turn "attributed to the caller" into "attributed to the step". Its cost has to be measured.
4. D42 says the bake-off servers keep their own copy, but `macro.sh` and `lab_start_server` start every server the same way (`lab/lib.sh:85-92`). Name the switch (an option, or `SQLITE_PATH` in `--env`). Otherwise a bake-off run gets a second 250 MB copy charged to its cgroup, and its `memory.current` no longer matches the bake-off's.
5. Scope 2's "a step far above capacity fails" at low load: with D39's scale (20,000 req/s at the cap), a step above capacity needs about the cap's VUs. Say how the smoke gets there: a tiny `--think-scale`, or a fixture server that spends a fixed CPU time per request (`details/phase-4.md:53`, `:166`).
6. D16 is missing a period before "D40 corrects the users" (`decisions.md:44`).

### [run, lab] : Guard D41's live stream at high rates

D41 sends k6's CSV (about 12 rows per request, about 240,000 rows/s at 20,000 req/s) through a FIFO to an aggregator.
If the aggregator falls behind, k6 buffers the samples in memory: no slower VUs, but k6's memory grows until D39's
limit kills k6. The plan watches the wrong sign (achieved rate at low load), and its fallback (one scenario per step)
streams just as much.

**Question:** what guards the stream?

- **A.** Three changes:
  - **Calibrate with the stream on:** k6's MiB per VU is measured with the CSV going into the FIFO.
  - **Prove the aggregator's speed:** a scope 2 case replays a recorded CSV at 2 × 20,000 req/s worth of rows.
  - **Gate every measured run:** the aggregator's lag (newest sample time against the clock) stays under 2 s and k6's memory stays under its budget, or the run is invalid with that reason.

  The fallback, if the replay fails, is a faster reader: it skips every line except `http_req_duration` and `http_reqs` before parsing, or uses `mawk`.

  **Aims at:** a failure that names the generator, never the server, and that shows up in scope 2, not scope 5.
  **For:** keeps D41's single scenario, and the check is cheap. **Against:** a replay fixture and a gate to write.
- **B.** Keep D41 as written, and watch k6's memory by hand in scope 5.
  **Aims at:** less code.
  **For:** nothing to build. **Against:** the failure shows up at the knee, on the quiet VM, as a k6 crash.

**Confidence:** A 85 % · B 15 %

**Recommendation:** A: it makes the generator fail with its own reason, and in scope 2 instead of in the measuring
scope.

**Flips if:** a first scope 2 run at the cap shows the aggregator at under 30 % of one CPU, with no lag and flat k6 memory (then the gate alone is enough, without the replay case).
